# Reservoir Drive Mechanisms

## What is a drive mechanism?

A reservoir drive mechanism is the natural source of energy that pushes fluids toward the wellbore during production. Understanding the dominant drive mechanism helps engineers predict pressure behaviour, production decline, and possible recovery.

## Main drive mechanisms

### 1. Solution gas drive
In this type of reservoir, gas is dissolved in the oil at high pressure. As pressure drops, gas comes out of solution and helps push oil toward the well.

Common features:
- pressure declines relatively quickly
- gas-oil ratio often increases after bubble point
- recovery can be limited without pressure support

### 2. Gas cap drive
A free gas cap above the oil zone expands as pressure falls. This expansion provides energy to support production.

Common features:
- better pressure support than pure solution gas drive
- gas cap management is important
- production strategy affects long-term recovery

### 3. Water drive
Water from an aquifer moves into the reservoir and displaces hydrocarbons toward producing wells.

Common features:
- relatively good pressure maintenance
- water production may increase over time
- sweep efficiency strongly affects recovery

### 4. Gravity drainage
Density differences allow fluids to segregate, with gas rising and water sinking while oil drains under gravity.

This is more effective when reservoir structure and permeability support vertical movement.

### 5. Compaction drive
As pressure declines, the reservoir rock compresses slightly and helps expel fluids.

This mechanism is less common as the main drive but can contribute in some reservoirs.

## Why drive mechanisms matter

Drive mechanisms affect:
- pressure decline rate
- gas-oil ratio trends
- water production behaviour
- recovery factor
- field development strategy

## Quick review

- Reservoir energy is the engine of production.
- Different drive mechanisms lead to different production patterns.
- Good reservoir management depends on identifying the dominant drive mechanism early.
