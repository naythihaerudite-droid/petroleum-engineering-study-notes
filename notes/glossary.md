# Petroleum Engineering Glossary

## A

**Artificial lift** — A method used to help fluids reach the surface when natural reservoir energy is not sufficient.

## B

**BOP (Blowout Preventer)** — A safety device used to control pressure and prevent uncontrolled flow from a well.

## C

**Casing** — Steel pipe installed in a well and cemented in place to support the wellbore and isolate formations.

## D

**Drive mechanism** — The natural energy source that pushes reservoir fluids toward the well.

## G

**Gamma ray log** — A log used to measure natural radioactivity and help distinguish shale from cleaner formations.

## H

**Hydrostatic pressure** — Pressure created by the weight of a fluid column.

## K

**Kick** — Entry of formation fluids into the wellbore due to insufficient well pressure.

## P

**Pay zone** — A formation interval capable of producing hydrocarbons economically.

**Permeability** — The ability of rock to allow fluids to flow through connected pore spaces.

**Porosity** — The fraction of rock volume that consists of pore space.

## R

**Recovery factor** — The proportion of hydrocarbons in place that can be produced.

**Reservoir** — A subsurface rock formation containing hydrocarbons in pore spaces.

**ROP (Rate of Penetration)** — The speed at which a drill bit advances through rock.

## S

**Saturation** — The fraction of pore space occupied by a particular fluid.

**Water cut** — The percentage of water in the total liquid produced from a well.

## W

**Well logging** — Measuring and recording formation properties in a well to support interpretation and decision-making.
