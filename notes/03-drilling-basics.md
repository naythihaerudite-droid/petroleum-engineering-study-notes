# Drilling Basics

## Purpose of drilling

Drilling creates a wellbore from the surface to the target formation. The objective may be exploration, appraisal, development, injection, or monitoring.

## Main components of a drilling system

### Drill bit
The bit cuts or crushes rock at the bottom of the hole.

### Drill string
The drill string transfers rotation, weight, and drilling fluid to the bit.

### Drilling fluid (mud)
Drilling fluid is essential for:
- carrying cuttings to the surface
- cooling and lubricating the bit
- controlling formation pressure
- stabilizing the wellbore
- forming filter cake

### Casing
Casing is steel pipe run into the well and cemented in place. It helps maintain well integrity and isolate formations.

## Important concepts

### Hydrostatic pressure
Mud exerts hydrostatic pressure. It must be high enough to control formation fluids but not so high that it fractures the formation.

### Kick
A kick occurs when formation fluids enter the wellbore because well pressure is too low. If not controlled, this can escalate into a blowout.

### Blowout preventer (BOP)
A BOP is a critical safety system used to seal, control, and monitor the well.

### Rate of penetration (ROP)
ROP is the speed at which the bit drills the formation, often measured in meters per hour or feet per hour.

## Drilling sequence (simplified)

1. Plan the well.
2. Spud the well.
3. Drill surface hole.
4. Run and cement casing.
5. Continue drilling intermediate and production sections.
6. Log and evaluate the well.
7. Reach total depth.
8. Prepare for completion or testing.

## Main drilling concerns

- well control
- stuck pipe
- lost circulation
- hole instability
- bit wear
- high cost of non-productive time

## Quick review

- Drilling is a controlled engineering process, not simply making a hole.
- Mud and casing are essential for safety and well integrity.
- Pressure control is one of the most important parts of drilling operations.
