# Well Logging Basics

## What is well logging?

Well logging is the measurement and recording of formation properties in a well. Logs help engineers and geoscientists identify lithology, fluid type, porosity, and possible hydrocarbon zones.

## Why logs are important

Logs are used to:
- identify reservoir intervals
- estimate porosity
- estimate water saturation
- correlate formations
- support completion decisions
- evaluate pay zones

## Common log types

### Gamma ray log
Measures natural radioactivity. It is often used to distinguish shale from cleaner formations like sandstone or limestone.

### Resistivity log
Measures how strongly a formation resists electric current. Hydrocarbon-bearing zones may show higher resistivity than water-bearing zones.

### Density log
Helps estimate porosity and lithology by measuring electron density related to bulk density.

### Neutron log
Responds mainly to hydrogen concentration and is commonly used for porosity estimation.

### Sonic log
Measures acoustic travel time through the formation. It can support porosity estimation and geomechanical analysis.

## Basic interpretation idea

No single log gives the full answer. Engineers usually combine multiple logs and compare them with core data, tests, and geological information.

Example:
- low gamma ray may suggest clean sand
- high resistivity may suggest hydrocarbons
- density-neutron response may support porosity interpretation

## Common caution

Log interpretation can be misleading if used alone. Shale effects, invasion, borehole conditions, and tool limitations must be considered.

## Quick review

- Logs provide indirect information about subsurface formations.
- Reliable interpretation comes from combining several types of data.
- Formation evaluation is about reducing uncertainty, not eliminating it completely.
