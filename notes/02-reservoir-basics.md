# Reservoir Basics

## What is a reservoir?

A petroleum reservoir is a subsurface rock formation that stores hydrocarbons in pore spaces. For production to happen, the rock must usually have both:
- porosity
- permeability

## Key terms

### Porosity
Porosity is the fraction of bulk rock volume that is pore space.

Formula:

```text
Porosity = Pore Volume / Bulk Volume
```

### Permeability
Permeability is the ability of rock to transmit fluids through connected pore spaces. It is commonly measured in darcies or millidarcies.

### Saturation
Saturation describes how much of the pore space is occupied by a specific fluid.
- water saturation (Sw)
- oil saturation (So)
- gas saturation (Sg)

In a simple system:

```text
Sw + So + Sg = 1
```

## Reservoir fluids

Reservoirs may contain:
- oil
- gas
- water

The distribution depends on pressure, temperature, density, and rock/fluid properties.

## Pressure and drive energy

Hydrocarbon production depends on reservoir energy. Common drive mechanisms include:
- solution gas drive
- gas cap drive
- water drive
- gravity drainage
- compaction drive

Each drive mechanism affects production behaviour and recovery factor.

## Recovery factor

Recovery factor is the percentage of hydrocarbons in place that can actually be produced.

```text
Recovery Factor = Recoverable Reserves / Hydrocarbons In Place
```

Not all oil or gas in the reservoir can be recovered because of rock properties, fluid behaviour, and operational limits.

## Why reservoir management matters

Reservoir engineers monitor pressure, production rate, water cut, gas-oil ratio, and other data to understand field performance. Good reservoir management helps:
- extend field life
- improve recovery
- reduce wasted investment
- support better well planning

## Quick review

- Reservoir rock stores fluids in pore spaces.
- Porosity tells how much space exists; permeability tells how easily fluids move.
- Pressure and drive mechanisms control production performance.
- Recovery factor is always less than 100% in real field conditions.
